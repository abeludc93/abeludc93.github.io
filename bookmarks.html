Interesting Bookmarks

A curated list of sites, videos, and resources that I find educational and worth sharing.

Programming
Resource Description
yet another insignificant Programming Notes A personal favourite containing programming notes and tutorials covering various languages (mostly Java) and concepts such as OOP, Databases, Webapps. Beginner friendly explanations from the ground-up. A must from Chua Hock-Chuan from the NTU University.
Material Curricular Libre Open educational resources and materials for web and software development (in Spanish). It also hosts a number of computer science magazines. Author: Bartolomé Sintes Marco.
Calmcode.io Short, clear, and calm video tutorials focusing on individual tools and libraries within the Python ecosystem. Comfy stuff. From Vincent D. Warmerdam, who's also a founding enginner of the much-beloved Marimo notebooks and whose personal blog (koaning.io) I also recommend.
MDN Web Docs T·H·E reference platform for consulting and learning standard web development.
CPSC 223: Data Structures and Programming Techniques This is a massive 2022 book from Yale. It covers all there is to know on both C programming and foundational data structures. I did not read the whole lot but it is quite useful for consulting thanks to its detailed table of contents - indexing around 800 pages.
Learn MATLAB: A Student's guide
Mathematics
Resource Description
HyperMath Concept maps and summary explanations of mathematical principles, applied statistics and their links to problems in physics and astronomy. Old but gold - © 1998 C.R. Nave from Georgia State University.
No Bullshit Textbooks A collection of books written by Ivan Savov, meant for students & self-learners. It styles a straight-to-the-point attitude on calculus, mechanics, and linear algebra. Upcoming releases include statistics, physics and an engineering guide. I've only read the linear algebra look so far, but I loved it. It's a huge book that covers many concepts: you really can't get more for that price. I recommend checking the first pages of his books online and downloading his printable tutorials for free.
Braver New Math Seth Braver - mathematics professor in the South Puget Sound Community College - has published a series of books with fresh perspectives on learning mathematics that I'm particulary fond of. I began with his 'The Dark Art of Linear Algebra: an intuitive geometric approach', but I have since read 'Full Frontal Calculus' and 'Precalculus Made Difficult', all of which are really exceptional. A simple, humoristic and unique style that speaks highly of its author. Currently reading his 'Lobachesky Illuminated' to shed some light on non-Euclidean geometry once and for all.
Penn Online Learning Calculus A good YouTube series on Single Variable Calculus from the University of Pennsylvania's by Prof. Robert Ghrist.
3Blue1Brown I've got nothing else to say: the best animated visual explanations of a multitude of mathematical concepts. Grant Sanderson single-handledly developed manim, a graphics library for creating mathematical animations. His famous YouTube channel is golden.
MatematIA Blog of Universidad de Sevilla professor Fernando Sancho Caparrini, with detailed entries on a suite of computer science topics, mathematics and modelling; a real treat. There are also full courses on logic, machine learning, multi-agent modelling and much more.
Cálculo en una variable (UDC) Collection of Jupyter notebooks covering single-variable calculus topics from Universidade da Coruña (in Spanish).
Cálculo en varias variables (UDC) Collection of Jupyter notebooks covering multivariable calculus topics from Universidade da Coruña (in Spanish).
Algebra I (UCLM) Course materials for Algebra I by Enrique Castillo at UCLM (available in English & Spanish).
Algebra II (UCLM) Course materials for Algebra II by Enrique Castillo at UCLM (available in English & Spanish).
Physics
Resource Description
HyperPhysics A broad and comprehensive exploration of different physics topics trough concept maps. © 2024 C.R. Nave from Georgia State University.
Javalab A rich collectin of interactive science simulations and virtual experiments for educational purposes. Technology and mathematics demos can also be found. As far as I know, this is an old website created in 1996 by South Korean teacher Dong-joon Lee. As the name suggest, the first simulations were actually Java applets, but they have been completely remade w/ HTML5.
Electric Circuits A free series of textbooks on the subjects of electricity and electronics. © 2000-2023, Tony R. Kuphaldt.
Circuit Simulator A useful, free and completely online electrical and electronic simulator written in JavaScript. (Paul Falstad / Iain Sharp)
Machine Learning
Resource Description
Applied Machine Learning in Python Excellent guide to machine learning by Andreas Müller, core contributor to scikit-learn, based on the course on ML that he held at Columbia, which I also recommend. It is a bottom-up free book which makes heavy use of notebooks and practices.
Applied Machine Learning (Columbia) You can find here the slides and video lectures for Andreas Müller's AML course at Columbia University. I recommend reading the slides or watching the corresponding video lectures before diving in the respective chapter from the previous link.
Machine Learning for Intelligent Systems Course site for CS4780 at Cornell University by professor Kilian Weinberger. This course may seem old nowadays (Fall 2018), but it contains great materials and video lectures on different machine learning topics.
Machine Learning and Pattern Recognition This is the homepage for Yann LeCun ML course at NYU (Fall 2010). I picked the last edition of said course, but there are available older versions from 2004. I've yet to review these, but it would be an interesting idea for a future post. Of course, practices involve LeCun's programming language, LUSH, a dialect of Lisp. I'll be covering these language, and some of the demos available, as well as a guide for its installation on modern machines in a future entry.
CS229: Machine Learning Stanford's legendary machine learning course taught by Andrew Ng. I've linked the current edition (Summer 2026). Search for the older Summer 2022 for Andrew's opera prima.
Introduction to Machine Learning Compendium of notes on ML basics by slama.dev (Tom) from Ullrich Köthe course at University of Heidelberg in 2022. I happen to known this webpage from this post, but I enjoyed his whole blog.
Cursos IA - Fernando Sancho Caparrini Teaching materials and courses on Artificial Intelligence, Logic, and Agent Modelling with Julia, NetLogo & Prolog (in Spanish). From University of Seville professor Fernando Sancho Caparrini, whose blog I also emphatically recommend.
Notas de Apredizaje Automático Profundo Notes of Machine Learning by Mariano Rivera, in Spanish (CIMAT © 2022, Mexican Mathematical Research Center).
Deep Learning
Resource Description
Neural networks and Deep learning The all-time classic 2015 online book by Michael Nielsen explaining the core principles of neural networks. A relatively short must-read for newcomers on DL.
Deep Learning Book Another classic on deep learning by legends Ian Goodfellow, Yoshua Bengio, and Aaron Courville (MIT Press). Online HTML format but printable. It is a good follow-up after Nielsen's Neural networks and Deep learning.
ML and DL courses Although I also list Alfredo Canziani entire webpage under Blogs & Educational Pages, I like to reference here directly his collection of courses on ML & DL that he generously uploads for free. Most of them are teaching materials from his NYU classes.
CS230: Deep Learning Another classic, Stanford's 2018 deep learning course taught by Andrew Ng. You can find slides on different topics under Syllabus and corresponding video-classes under Lecture Videos.
Neural Network Design I encountered this book -by Hagan, Demuth, Beale & De Jesús- after firing up the Neural Networks section on the demo app of Matlab 5.0 of all places. I was baffled with the quality of simulations and interactive figures that the team had created in order to promote their book. Later, I was delighted to know that the same authors created a Python package that ports those same demos, called nndesigndemos. Furthermore, they are working of a follow-up book, focusng of deep neural networks. All code and textbooks are freely available online.
Sebastian Raschka's Courses Sebastian is a former professor in the department of Statistics at the UWM and is better known for his excellent free video lectures, notebooks and courses on both machine learning - but specially - on deep learning. I recommend his famous Build a Large Languague Model (From Scratch) as a good in-depth intro to LLMs.
The Little Book of Deep Learning A compact, phone-formatted introduction to deep learning by François Fleuret. It is relatively brief but it cuts straight to the point. It's a nice resource to have at hand and as a quick look-up for consultation.
Practical Deep Learning for Coders Jeremy Howard, cofounder of Fast.ai and ex-president at Kaggle, teaches this renowned top-down course to deep learning, with an examples first approach, consisting of 9 lessons of hour and a half each, plus additional & freely available material. I personally did not watch this series yet, but I will let Peter Norvig (Researh Director @ Google) convice you: ... this is one of the best sources for a programmer to become proficient in deep learning.. To finish off, they have recently released a second part focusing of teaching Stable Diffusion.
Understanding Deep Learning Online book providing a modern overview of deep learning concepts, such as Transformers, LLMs, NLP, CV, RL, but also ML & Optimization theory. By Simon J.D. Prince. I did not review this material yet but it strikes me as good quality. Also, for those a hands-on approach, there are lots of code notebooks available (68!) with examples covering the whole text.
Data Science
Resource Description
Statistics and Data Science A jupyter book on data science and statistics by Prof. Cranmer of NYU Physics, conceived as a graduate-level course. Although the recorded lectures are not available (unless you're a student of NYU), there collection of notebooks on different topics such as probability, statistics and ML are wonderful.
Ciencia de datos Free divulgative web offering theory and practical examples in both R and Python on statistics, forecasting and data science topics. Available primarily in Spanish, but also English and Chinese. Great team!
Modelling & Simulation
Resource Description
Nathan Kutz Lecture materials and videos on applied math, focused on data-driven methods, by Nathan Kutz and Steven Brunton, from the University of Washington. This is a magnificient resource, feel free to explore Nathan's website for more material. (example: book connecting data science with dynamical systems and control Data Driven Science and Engineering).
Steven Brunton It's Eigensteve. His YouTube channel alone is worth a gold mine. He has video courses on pretty much everything related with modelling and data science: engineering maths, dynamical systems, ML, RL, PhysicsML, control theory, statistics & probability... you name it. -I'M A BIG FAN-.
Cleve Moler's Textbooks Co-author of the LINPACK & EISPACK libraries, founder and chief scientist of MathWorks©, Cleve Moler was MATLAB. The folks at MathWorks have two of his textbooks online, alongside course materials: Numerical Computing with MATLAB, and Experiments with MATLAB.
Belt Of Orion Great STEM articles on Mathematics, Physics, and Computer Science (example: numerical comparison using a pendulum Runge Kutta vs Euler). Sadly, it does not have a large collection of posts, but every one of them is worth reading!
Digital Signals Theory Online book on digital signal processing that follows the course syllabus for Fundamentals of Digitals Signals Theory (I) by NYU professor Brian McFee.
Modelling and Scientific Computing in Python An online course on modelling and scientific computing with Python created for the Oxford Doctoral Training Centre by Martin Robinson.
Scientific Computing in MATLAB A brief online course on scientific computing using MATLAB. It's not quite analogous to the previous one, but it's shorter and nicely put. Created by Martin Robinson for the Oxford Doctoral Training Centre.
Blogs
Resource Description
Peter Norvig Personal site of AI pioneer and research director at Google Peter Norvig. Co-author with Stuart Russell of the must-read book Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach. I also really enjoy his short tutorials (for instance, Lispy: Scheme Interpreter in Python) and IAQ posts.
Yann LeCun Home page of the Turing Award winner and Chairman of AMI Labs. Apart from a large collection of free courses on ML and Pattern Recognition based directly on courses he taught on the late 2000's (TEACHING), he's also a fellow model airplane enthuasist. Finally, I recommend checking out his Lush and DjVu software projects, I'll write a future post talking about them.
Alfredo Canziani Personal webpage of Alfredo Canziani, professor of Computer Science at the NYU, and mentored by Yann LeCun. He's devoted to free education and I've got huge respect for that. He's posted several of his DL and ML courses online, with video lectures and materials under the Didactics page. He's also finish a major book called Deep Learning with an Energy perspective, which I'm eager to read. I recommend following him on Twitter/GitHub to stay updated.
Fabrice Bellard Prolific and discrete programmer, author of tools susch as FFmpeg, QEMU, tcc, and many-many others. His webpage is a personal laboratory with all of his creations: a must-visit. Want to run a full-fledged operating system in your webbrowser?
Cleve Moler's Blog Blog of Creator of MATLAB and founder of MathWorks©, Cleve Moler, on scientific computing, maths and history. All good-reads.
Enrique Castillo Academic courses on algebra, optimization, statistics and engineering applications materials from the excellent professor and member of the Royal Academy of Sciences of Spain, Enrique Castillo. (resources in both English & Spanish)
Juergen Schmidhuber Home page of the co-creator of LSTM (and many other DL networks such as adversarial networks GAN and unnormalised linear transformers). All his papers are milestones in the field.
Colah's Blog Who else besides an AI interpretability expert could make such incredible visual explanations and representations of neural networks? Anthropic co-founder, and Google Brain & OpenAI researcher Christopher Olah. I believe his most recent posts are hosted over at Distill. Both sites are worth checking out!
Lil'Log Profound, high-quality technical dives into DL and AI architectures since 2017 from now OpenAI technical staff Lilian Weng. Good reads.
Thore Graepel Webpage I've recently come across the interesting and resourceful webpage of Thore Grapel, a lifelong ML researcher and DeepMind member who worked within the AlphaGo team. Lots of fascinating posts on reasoning, and overall an insider view of someone who worked on the first program ever that won the best professional Go player.
Vincent Warmerdam's Blog Blog from calmcode creator and data-person Vincent. He currently works at marimo, which are a kind of live and reactive Jupyter networks. I love his work.
Andreas Müller ML professor and scikit-learn core contributor's personal webpage. He's also the author of O'Reillys' book Introduction to Machine Learning with Python, which is the recommended material for his Columbia and NYU teaching courses on Applied Machine Learning that are now freely availalabe online (video lectures and slides).
Eamonn J. Keogh Webpage of distinguished professor of the University of California Eamonn Keogh, known for his work on the Matrix Profile, a technique for time series data mining. He hosts tutorials, publications and news related to time series classification, anomaly detection and clustering. It's worthwhile checking it out!
Romain Tavenard Professor at Université Rennes focused on time-series analysis and machine learning. I've linked directly to his lectures based on course materials of DL & ML for time series. Great stuff!
Tung Nguyen I can't remember exactly how I found out about this research blog, but I liked it. It is a portfolio of a post-doctoral fellow at the University of Manchester. Under the programming tab he hosts multiple Python tutorials on ML concepts.
slama.dev Tomáš Sláma webpage. Similar to the previous case, I think I found this blog because of some his notes on GANs, ML and the manim graphics library. Besides, he has a great YouTube channel, but his notes based on course materials from his Master's in Heidelberg are excellent.
Visual Rambling I might start a new miscellaneous section with this one, but for now it stays here. A fascinating and wonderfully crafted exploration of dithering graphics algorithms.
Retro-computing
Resource Description
The Old Net A portal to browse the early web exactly as it was in the 90s and 00s. As you may guess, it uses the Internet Archive as a proxy. Nonetheless, it has a great collection of old classic and random webpages. Fun stuff.
ProtoWeb A free proxy service that hosts restored historical internet sites. The difference with the previous service is that they cured and restored each website to the smallest detail, so when you redirect an old machine (virtual or not) webbrowser with it, it really feels like a time machine. The downside is that they have a shorter list of restores sited, defaulting to the Internet Archive versions otherwise.
Coronet Films List of Coronet Films - an archive of old educational films. I enjoy watching these films. I've yet to prepare a good collection on PBS educational goldies.
Internet Archive Probably the most important webpage on the planet. No need for extra comments.
